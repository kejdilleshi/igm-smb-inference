\begin{abstract}
The distributed surface mass balance (SMB) of mountain glaciers is a key forcing
for projecting glacier evolution and subsequent meltwater availability. Yet,
direct in situ observations cover less than 0.2\,\% of the world's glacierised
area. Here, we present a new SMB inference method, implemented within the
open-source Instructed Glacier Model (IGM), that recasts the problem as a fully
differentiable transient inversion. We first assimilate observed surface ice
speeds at one snapshot, then run a forward simulation between two epochs of known
surface elevation under a candidate SMB. Automatic differentiation through the
forward run yields the gradient of a geometry-misfit cost function with respect
to the SMB, enabling gradient-based optimisation of the latter. We apply the
method to three glaciers in the Alps---Argentière, Great Aletsch and Rhône---and
validate the inferred profiles against in situ stake measurements, outperforming
the distributed reconstruction of Kneib and others (2024) on Argentière. A
sensitivity test shows that the surface-velocity misfit alone bounds the inferred
SMB on stake-free glaciers. Our method is generic, computationally efficient and
requires no glacier-specific tuning, making it readily transferable to any
glacier with accurate multi-temporal DEMs and surface velocities, opening a
path toward regional-scale SMB inversion.
\end{abstract}
